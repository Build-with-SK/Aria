How to read this

  • The evaluation universe rose in 52%-78% of the measured windows. A module that mostly calls BEAR is penalised by hit-rate-versus-base-rate on a sample like this even when its bearish calls beat chance — read bear_hit_rate and the information coefficient for those, not edge_over_base.
  • Eight large-cap US names over roughly ten years is one market and one decade. These figures do not transfer to small caps, to other geographies, or to a decade that falls.
  • Most modules are rule-based rather than fitted, so this is out-of-sample across time, not trained-here-tested-there. The rules were written by someone who had lived through this period, which no walk-forward can undo.
  • Modules flagged point_in_time=false read the wall clock and so were not perfectly replayed; their figures are indicative only.
  • Hit rate above 50% is not skill on its own. The market rose in most of the measured windows, so the honest comparison is the "vs base" column.
  • An edge marked * is not statistically significant — it is inside the noise for the sample behind it, and should be read as "no measurable effect", not as a small one.
  • The "Independent" column is the call count after discounting for names that move together. Forty tickers on the same date are forty observations of one market day, not forty facts. It is usually a lot smaller than "Calls", and it is the number the statistics are actually built on.
  • Everything here is paper. No number on this page was produced by risking money.
